Research

We study how people remember, focus, decide, and work with AI. We use research to create product hypotheses, not scientific-looking marketing.

Read research notesResearch data

How we use evidence

A paper can show an effect in one setting. It does not prove that the same feature will help every Wooolfmesh user. We separate the research result from our product idea, then test the idea in the app.

What we care about

We focus on practical questions: How can capture reduce mental load? How can focus survive interruptions? How can old lessons return at the right time? How much control should an AI agent have? Which signals help without creating another dashboard?

Evidence labels

Established: supported by a review, meta-analysis, or repeated research.

Strong signal: useful experimental or field evidence.

Emerging: recent evidence that needs more replication.

Research notes

Each note ends with something concrete we can test in Wooolfmesh.

EstablishedCognition

Cognitive offloading

People often move information into external tools to reduce the amount they must keep active in their mind. Reminders and notes are simple examples.

For Wooolfmesh: Capture should be extremely fast. Structure can come later. The system should remember commitments, context, and next steps so the user does not have to keep rehearsing them mentally.

Test: a one-action capture inbox with later clarification, plus a visible “you can forget this now” confirmation.

Risko & Gilbert, 2016
Strong signalAttention

Attention residue after switching

When people leave unfinished work, part of their attention can stay with the previous task. This can reduce performance on the next task.

For Wooolfmesh: Focus should protect the active task, but it should also make interruptions safe to capture without opening a new work stream.

Test: interruption capture inside Focus, followed by a short closeout: outcome, next action, and the exact point to resume.

Leroy, 2009
EstablishedAction

If-then plans help action

Implementation intentions connect a situation with a planned response: “If X happens, I will do Y.” A large meta-analysis found a meaningful positive effect on goal achievement.

For Wooolfmesh: routines can be more useful when they are linked to clear triggers instead of only fixed schedules.

Test: local rules such as “If a focus session ends with an open blocker, add it to tomorrow’s review.” Always preview durable changes first.

Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006
EstablishedMemory

Retrieval can strengthen memory

Trying to recall information can improve later retention more than simply reading the same material again.

For Wooolfmesh: cognitive bites should not become another archive. The system can sometimes ask the user to recall a lesson before revealing it.

Test: an optional “Recall first” review card for important lessons, decisions, or mistakes linked to current work.

Roediger & Karpicke, 2006
Strong signalHuman + AI

AI changes critical thinking

A CHI 2025 study of knowledge workers found that higher confidence in generative AI was linked with less reported critical-thinking effort. The work also showed that critical thinking moves toward verification, integration, and supervision.

For Wooolfmesh: AI should not only produce answers. It should make verification easy and keep the user responsible for important decisions.

Test: show sources, confidence, assumptions, and “verify before apply” cues for higher-impact AI suggestions.

Lee et al., CHI 2025
Strong signalAI UX

How AI enters the workflow matters

A CHI 2025 experiment found different cognitive patterns when AI led a task, co-led it, or was absent. More automation was not simply “better”; different modes changed comprehension, revision, confidence, and idea diversity.

For Wooolfmesh: one giant AI answer is not always the right interface. Some workflows may work better when AI gives one step, question, or suggestion at a time.

Test: a co-led task shaping mode that asks one useful question before generating a full plan.

CHI 2025: Productive vs. Reflective
Strong signalAgents

Plan first, execute second

A CHI 2025 study of daily-assistant agents found that user involvement and plan quality both matter. Plausible-looking plans can still create misplaced trust.

For Wooolfmesh: agent work should be inspectable before execution. The user should be able to review the plan, then approve bounded steps.

Test: Plan → Preview → Approve → Execute for agent workflows, with visible checkpoints and a local action log.

Plan-Then-Execute, CHI 2025
EmergingAgent autonomy

Medium autonomy may be a useful middle ground

A 2026 productivity-agent study compared manual work with medium and high autonomy. Both agent modes reduced workload, while medium autonomy gave the best balance between productivity and user preference for control in that study.

For Wooolfmesh: the future may not be “manual or fully autonomous.” Different actions can have different autonomy levels based on risk and user choice.

Test: allow pre-approved low-risk local actions while keeping durable or destructive actions behind explicit confirmation.

Geninatti Cossatin et al., 2026

Research backlog

Ideas worth testing

These are hypotheses, not promised features.

Resume points

After focus or interruption, save the exact next physical action and the context needed to restart.

Trigger-based routines

Support if-then rules based on local events, not only clock time.

Recall before reveal

For selected cognitive bites, ask for a short memory attempt before showing the stored lesson.

AI confidence gates

Make uncertainty, evidence, and approval more visible when AI suggestions can change durable work memory.

Co-led AI

Test AI that asks one strong question or offers one next step instead of producing a complete plan immediately.

Adaptive autonomy

Let users choose which low-risk actions can run automatically and which always require approval.
